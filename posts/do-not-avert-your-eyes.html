Do not avert your eyes.

Being a rumination on geekery and essays offered on October 5, 2026

CW and Disclosures

Content Warning: Discussion of Jacob Geller and, therefore, sexual assault.

First, positionality. I’m a cishet white guy in a position of immense privilege. I’m a d-tier YouTuber but I am a very good essayist, which is what my stake and standing here is. I have never been a victim of sexual assault, nor have I ever been a perpetrator of sexual assault. My entire history of violence against women is that when I was 13, I slapped a girl my age in an adolescent conflict that could not even really be explained at the time. I will return to that in this piece, and I have attempted to reckon with it in my writing before, but I disclose it now to say if you view that, or anything else I have disclosed here or below as disqualifying, you are extremely welcome to not read this. I might also view it as disqualifying, if not for the fact that there has been a lot of conversation that seems to be missing a big important thing, and in the absence of someone better saying it, I’m going to say it.

Second, an apology. We are all talking abstractly about a concrete thing that was done to a specific person. Whoever she is, Geller’s victim has had no voice in any of this. I hope she is not listening. If you are, I do not know you, but I am sorry.

A lot has been said about Jacob Geller,1 and the nature and mystery of restorative justice, of what his victim is owed and of what we can and cannot know about the crime he committed, about the secret workings of his heart and whether his admission of guilt (such as it is) constitutes adequate self-recrimination for us to keep watching his videos.

The question which is being batted around, with various levels of care and sincerity, is this: has Jacob Geller atoned? If we take Geller at his word (which is an enormous “if”), he made amends with the victim, turned himself in to the authorities, accepted the consequences (“very mild, I concur”), and has spent the last decade making up for his misdeed by—checks notes—making YouTube videos about videogames. This version of the story assumes a ton of facts not in evidence; in fact, each element of that story is tangibly unsupported even by his own narrative.2 But for some voices in the conversation, it has been ten years, during which Geller has comported himself admirably; is that not what restorative justice means?

There is, I think, a deeper flaw in this logic than the minor inconvenience of all of its parts being wrong. Because this framing is asking about whether Geller has atoned for what he did in 2016. I cannot answer this question, nor can anyone other than his victim, who has wanted no part in this conversation. That is also, I think, not the wrongdoing that is actually at issue here.

The reason we are discussing this now, the injustice that has us all spinning, is not the sexual assault that Jacob Geller committed in 2016. That is the greatest of his transgressions, and it is plenty to reckon with on its own. It is horrible, and it is haunting, but it is not what we are dealing with now. The reason that we are having this conversation is not the wrong thing Jacob Geller did in 2016. It is the wrong thing he has been doing, constantly and pervasively, for the last ten years.

We are not victims of Geller’s assault. But all of us, even those who just watched his videos once in a while, have been lied to about something essential. We have not all been hurt in the same way, nor to the same extent, and none of us suffered even a fraction of what his victim in 2016 did. But that doesn’t mean we haven’t been harmed.

a lit review in a blog post, why are we all like this

To my mind, we have to separate the harm caused by Geller’s initial assault in 2016, which happened to one person in 2016, and which has absolutely not a single thing to do with any of us, and the harm caused by the ten years he has spent hiding it while ascending to video essay godhood on the wings of his image as a safe feminist man.

In “Consider the woodchipper,” Will Borger says in very strong language that we are not Geller’s victims:

Let me be clear: you are not a victim of what Geller did. You watched a YouTube video (or several) and believed in a public persona of goodness crafted by a man who has none. This is not about you, and framing it that way is disturbing. You should consider why you need this to be about you. You may have your own personal response to Geller and his work, but there is no “we.” There is just each of us, and how we deal with it ourselves.

What I think he intends to say is not what he is actually saying, because he proves himself wrong in spectacular fashion a few paragraphs down (hold that thought). What I think he intends to say is this: Jacob Geller did not sexually assault you. To treat ourselves as a victim to that crime is, I agree, unacceptable. But I think Borger is wrong to say that there is no “we.” If there were no “we,” we would not be having this conversation right now.

Flora Merigold similarly brings up and lays aside this question. Or rather, she asserts obliquely that we are Geller’s victims, but that it maybe doesn’t matter?

Geller dug his own grave by not disclosing his past actions to his current collaborators and audience.

This sentence stands out starkly from the essay around it, to the extent that I keep thinking it is perhaps a quote or a position she is summarizing. But it does not seem to be. The remainder of the essay is focused on the question of whether Geller has adequately atoned for the sexual assault he committed at UNC. But the thing Merigold and Borger both seem to acknowledge without acknowledging is that that is not the only harm that Geller has done here. Geller has committed one crime3 that we are aware of. He has not as far as anyone knows gone faced criminal prosecution for that crime, and the consequences he did face were, in his own words, “very mild.”4 But for the moment let us imagine, as some of Geller’s defenders seem so powerfully to want to do, that Geller has behaved with pure moral perfection since the moment of his crime. Let us say he has atoned for the crime he committed in 2016.5

Does that mean no further harm has been done?

Some specific, tangible harms worth considering

Of course not. The reason I am writing this—with, at the moment, the intention of sharing it only in the private spaces where these conversations have taken place—is that people I know are struggling with the fact that they feel hurt, they feel lied to, they feel betrayed, and they feel in some cases unwelcome in their own community. And they do not know how to deal with it, because very obviously, they are not the victims of Jacob Geller’s 2016 sexual assault. So why do they feel like this?

The answer is that they are among the victims of Geller’s second wrongdoing, which is incomparably smaller in significance but much wider in scale hiding what he had done from his audience and collaborators. This does not compare in any meaningful way to the significance of his actual crime, but it is real, and it does matter, and it is not wrong of us to think of ourselves as having been harmed.

Merigold and Borger both know this. Borger in fact enumerates this specifically:

Did Geller inform the folks at Cane and Rinse of what he had done? No. How about the people at MinnMax? No. Lost in Cult? No. Nebula? No. None of those organizations, or the people within them, agreed to platform or work with a man who committed, by his own admission, an act of sexual violence that was, at one point, a matter of public record. Not one.

So, just to establish the principle, there has been financial harm attached to Geller’s failure to disclose. All of those organizations had past partnerships with Geller, and all of them immediately cut ties once Geller’s crime came to their attention. As I understand it, Geller was a huge draw for Nebula and a significant earner for the platform. That likely represents a substantial loss of income for Nebula as a platform. Lost in Cult has pulled Geller’s upcoming book from production and started issuing refunds. This is after a ton of work has already gone into the product. Artists and writers have been paid; copy has been edited; the guy who comes up with the tiny numbers that go on the copyright page has been working for months. I imagine that they have already paid Geller an advance that they will not likely see again.

I know less about MinnMax or Cane and Rinse, but it seems obvious here that all of these people entered into collaboration with Geller under false pretenses. If they had known, they would not have worked with him. Because he hid what had happened, they now are making financial sacrifices that will potentially affect a whole bunch of people’s income and livelihoods. Can Nebula stay afloat without Geller? Can Lost in Cult afford to refund all of their preorders, given that presumably the preorder money was already spent on production? I don’t know. But it’s not at all out of the question that people will get laid off because Geller was dishonest.

But the financial harm is, if I’m honest, not the most important thing about this second wrongdoing. It is the hurt and betrayal I have seen coming from literally everyone who I have seen associated with him online. Lola Sebastian and Laura Crone both spoke to the betrayal they felt as people who suddenly had to account for their past association with this man, and both speak eloquently and powerfully about their own work writing about sexual violence, about their conscious decision to collaborate with Geller as, in Sebastian’s words, “a vocal feminist” and “a creator who was known to be a trustworthy ally to women and survivors of sexual violence,” and their betrayal and panic when they discovered that this person they had invited into their work was, in fact, not worthy of that trust. If you are on BlueSky, you have seen any number of people posting through it; I could link to a dozen creators, but I am most in awe of Ian Danskin of Innuendo Studios, who is still in the replies on Geller’s original thread carefully, patiently, laboriously explaining to people that no, in fact, sexual assault is not okay as long as you make good youtube videos after. Some have taken down videos, many have had to make public statements, and none of them—literally none of them that I have seen—have said, “yes, Jacob disclosed this to me and I decided to work with him anyway.” Every single one, as far as I can tell, worked with him only because he hid what he had done.

But even these people, who have in fact been harmed by Geller’s failure to disclose, are not the only victims of this second wrongdoing.

A hypothetical (but not-that-hypothetical) harm I can’t stop thinking about

In his video about The Last Guardian, Geller gestured in the vaguest possible terms at how the consequences of his crime (again, such as they were) had affected him. Of course, he did not mention the crime part. Here is what he says:

A few years ago, I was in a really rough spot. I had moved back home and I was working part time in a restaurant, and I had through my own actions isolated myself from like 99% of the people who had been in my life, and in my search for reasons to, like, keep…being a person who did anything meaningful, I got a dog!

I’m not gonna link the video, but if you want to watch this section, qvotoms on bsky has clipped it. There is sad piano music.

I will note that, to my reading, the acknowledgment of wrongdoing on Geller’s part is ambiguous at best. “Through my own actions” means he made a choice, but when I heard those words for the first time I did not for an instant assume that this was an allusion to a crime had committed. But of course, now we know that it was. The content of the text is this: I was in a dark place, I got a dog, and it helped.

When I say that Geller’s decision to hide what he had done has a broad range of victims, this is what I mean. He is speaking obliquely here about something that, if he spoke plainly, would have caused his audience to recoil. Viciously and immediately. But he chooses language that obfuscates the significance of what he did, handwaves it away not only so as to make it seem like his crime wasn’t a big deal, but to give us no particular reason to think he’d done anything wrong at all.

When I first rewatched this clip after having found out what Geller did, an imaginary person sprung full-formed into my head, and I cannot stop thinking about her.

The woman I am imagining was a victim of sexual assault. She was not believed by her peers or supported by her institution. And so she found herself alone, lost, and depressed. In a dark place. Isolated. And she watched this video, and she thought yes. That will help. I will get a dog.

The question I keep asking myself is, how does this woman feel about that dog now? Is it possible for her to feed her dog without thinking about Geller, and what he did, and her assaulter, and what she suffered?

Again, I made this woman up, and this hypothetical is pretty on the nose. But here’s the thing: just because I made her up doesn’t mean she isn’t real.

I am in a lot of video essayist communities. Some are fan spaces for various creators, including my own odd little Patreon server. Others are collaborative spaces for working video essayists. All of them have been knocked ass over teakettle by this revelation, and all of them are trying to make sense of it.

Many of them cannot engage with any of it without also having to renavigate their own sexual assault or abuse. The number of long messages in spoiler tags with content warnings I have seen is truly immiserating. And while, again, it does not compare in any meaningful way to the harm Geller did to his victim, this is still real harm. The people who are being triggered, retraumatized, forced to sit there and make sense of a world where their favorite youtuber just brought the worst thing that happened to them in their entire life right back into the room with him while he’s talking about fucking fungus in videogames or whatever, that is real harm.

There are others I have seen who are looking at this from the other side. Some of us are looking at what has happened to Geller in the last few days and thinking, I have done bad things in the past. Is this how I should be treated? This is, in part, why I started by disclosing my own past before I even started. I have done bad things in the past. Not, I think, on the scale of Geller. But I understand why some might look at the consequences Geller is facing and think “does anything I have done to atone for my own failures matter?” This is, I think, also harm—though of a different kind. If you are one of the people who has shared a story like this, please, please hear this: you are already more honest than Jacob Geller, you are already working harder at being better than Jacob Geller, and you are already succeeding better than Jacob Geller, because you are not lying about it. I thank you for that.

The problem, as I have been suggesting, is that we are treating two misdeeds as if they were the same. There is the sexual assault that Jacob Geller committed in approximately 2016. We should not take him at his word that it’s cool, bro, we worked it out. Obviously. But even if we pretend we did, even if we stipulate that yes, restorative justice happened (which again we do not fucking know a single thing about), if we imagine that that crime was somehow atoned for, it doesn’t matter a single bit to what is happening now. That is not the misdeed for which Geller is currently facing consequences.

He is facing consequences because hiding what he did from his collaborators and audience caused harm independent from his initial crime.

…but what was he supposed to doooooooooooo?

I have heard this a lot, usually with some sarcastic absurdity attached. “What did you expect him to do, start every video with ‘hi, i’m a rapist?’”

I think that is a very silly way to think about honesty. Like be for real. This is the same as saying that we should all start every conversation by disclosing when we pooped last. No, I do not think Geller should have done that. I think he should have been fucking honest.

In 2011, I found myself playing God of War III, and in the manner of my people, I also found myself writing about it. Essayists are horribly predictable in this way. What I was writing about was how the violence Kratos did seemed to mean nothing. And so I did what I think any essayist worth their salt should: I told the fucking truth.

When I mentioned above that I had reckoned with my own past history of violence in my writing before, I meant it as literally as I can. If you want to know more detail about what happened, you can read that essay. I published it in Kill Screen, which is now defunct, but it’s on the Wayback Machine. You can read the whole thing, as best I remembered it 15 years ago, which is better than I remember it now.[65] Because the most essential rule of being an essayist is this: do not avert your eyes. If you are looking at yourself, and you see a thing you want to hide or shy away from, you have to fucking stare at it and wrestle it onto the page or else the essay you are making is meaningless.

I don’t talk about what I did when I was 13 in every essay. It’s usually not relevant, even obliquely. But if there is a part of my experience that matters to the essay I am making, I have to face it. That is what being an essayist is. If I don’t do that, I am a liar, because the mind I am showing you in my essay is not real. It has no integrity.

In many of the spaces where I have discussed this, many of my friends and fellow essayists—including people who are just viewers, for the most part, now thrust into essay in long discord messages— attempt to drag themselves across the field of broken glass that Geller has strewn around us all. I have seen people cry in voice calls. I have seen friends and strangers share their trauma and recount their own past misdeeds as their to make sense of what this situation says about who they are.

And all of those people, without having to consider deeply or go through any extra effort, have included in a part of that conversation their positionality. We can’t talk about something this big without having our own experiences inform and interfere with how we think, and so we have to foreground who we are in relation to the question. Some people chose to identify themselves as SA survivors. Others disclosed their own wrongdoings, however vaguely, as a part of explaining why the rehabilitation aspect of the conversation hit them so hard.

And, as referenced in the angry pep talk I gave on bsky the other day, I’ve seen a lot of people unsure if they can continue to be essayists, knowing what Geller is. That, too, is a harm he has done. Every essayist who gives up making art because of this is a loss we all suffer.

It does not compare to the literal, physical and emotional harm he did to his victim. That cannot escape our attention. These other harms I am laying out do not eclipse or even approach the scope of his actual crime. We are not victims of that crime.

But through Geller’s refusal to introspect, to speak honestly and vulnerably about the things he did in the moments when it would have mattered, he has done harm to a lot of people. Some of it is financial, some of it is emotional. People are losing friends. Communities are in turmoil. I’ve seen a number of people referring to Geller as a pillar of the video essay community, and they’re not wrong. And when a pillar fails, the structure is at risk of collapse.

why am i still writing this

Because of you, maybe. Because I got so mad at what Borger said, that “you are not a victim of what Geller did,” that “there is no ‘we’.” Because that is self-evidently false. “We” are absolutely not the victim of Jacob Geller’s sexual assault. But that doesn’t mean he hasn’t harmed us.
